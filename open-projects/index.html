Starting out

These open projects are proposed by professors and industry partners. The ideas below can serve as a starting point for deeper reflection, and we encourage students to draw inspiration from them to shape their own personal projects.

Of course, new ideas are always welcome, and we are here to help you find the right supervisor for your project. If you are interested in any of these projects, want to know more details, or wish to propose your own, please contact us and we will guide you through the next steps.

Project philosophy

Projects are developed during the semester, laying the essential groundwork for discovery. To achieve the most significant breakthroughs, we highly recommend continuing your work as a summer lab internship. This is the best way to push your project forward.

For those getting started, we suggest focusing on a binder design project first. This will give you the essential skills needed to confidently tackle more complex or out-of-the-box protein engineering ideas later on.

Before you join

Please carefully read the expectations for newcomers before signing up to any project.

How to reach us

Write to dlai@epfl.ch with the project that interests you, or tell us about the idea you would like to pursue.

what the labs
are proposing

Our projects span AI‑driven sequence design, structure prediction, and experimental validation in collaboration with EPFL labs. Each group below provides scientific guidance, lab space and a real design challenge.

Found one you like? Email us and join a project.

Bruno Correia and his team propose

5 projects
  • Design of artificial converters of chemical energy into molecular motion
  • Making proteins see the light — design chromophore binding proteins
  • Design of protein prosthetics — rewiring deficient protein interactions in cells
  • Exporting membrane protein activities to the soluble proteome
  • Computational design of protein-based chaperones to correct protein stability defects

Sahand Rahi and his team propose

3 projects
  • Inhibit and visualise previously untargetable intra-cellular processes by designing conformation-specific binders — for example, age-detecting proteins
  • Target cancer-specific epitopes — for example, cancer-specific surface markers
  • Design single-protein computers — for example, AND/OR logic gating in a single protein

Matteo Dal Peraro and his team propose

5 projects
  • Designing artificial enzymes for plastic degradation and other sustainability tasks
  • Developing specific sensors for lipid recognition
  • Exploring the role of dynamics for protein design
  • Designing functional RNA molecules
  • Designing nanopores for molecular sensing

Patrick Barth and his team propose

4 projects
  • De novo design of GPCRs
  • De novo design of opto-switchable binders that regulate cell function
  • De novo design of opto-switchable ion channels
  • De novo design of antigen-binding domains for next generation Chimeric Antigen Receptors

Paolo De Los Rios and his team propose

1 project
